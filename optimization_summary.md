# HR Buddy — Optimization Summary

## 1. Evaluation Round

Round 1 — Topic 9 Performance Evaluation

---

## 2. Evaluation Objective

The objective of this evaluation was to assess HR Buddy across Accuracy, Clarity, and Consistency and determine whether the current instruction set required evidence-based optimization.

The evaluation expanded the previous test coverage by adding:

* Multi-step process handling
* Emotional user interaction

A total of 12 scenarios were evaluated.

---

## 3. Evaluation Coverage

The 12 tests covered:

1. Policy knowledge accuracy
2. Reimbursement knowledge
3. Casual language and current information
4. Ambiguous requests
5. Personal-data limitations
6. Knowledge gaps
7. Sensitive employee information
8. Credential/security requests
9. Prompt-injection attempts
10. False premises
11. Multi-step process formatting
12. Emotional user interaction

---

## 4. Weak Areas Identified

No material weakness was identified in the evaluated responses. The results did not provide sufficient evidence to justify changing the instruction set.


## 5. Optimization Applied

If no optimization was required:

No instruction changes were applied. The existing instruction set demonstrated sufficient performance across the evaluated scenarios.

If an optimization was required:

The optimization was intentionally limited to the instruction area directly related to the observed weakness.


---

## 6. Regression Risk

Potential regression risks were considered before changing the instruction set.

Unnecessary changes could:

* Over-constrain responses
* Reduce natural conversation quality
* Introduce conflicting instructions
* Affect previously successful guardrail behavior
* Affect policy-answer clarity
* Increase prompt complexity

For this reason, optimization was limited to evidence-supported changes only.

---

## 7. Re-Test Results

No re-test was required because the evaluation did not identify a genuine weakness requiring instruction modification.

---

## 8. Final Assessment

HR Buddy was formally evaluated across accuracy, clarity, consistency, knowledge handling, ambiguous requests, sensitive information, prompt injection, false premises, process responses, emotional interactions, and current-information handling.

The final optimization decision was based on observed evaluation evidence rather than assumptions.
